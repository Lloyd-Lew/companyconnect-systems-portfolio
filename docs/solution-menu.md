# Solution Menu: Choose the Right Starting Point

The right engagement depends on how clear the operational problem is, how many systems are involved, and whether the business has a workable plan. This guide prevents two common mistakes: starting a large build before priorities are clear, or buying a narrow automation when the real problem spans sales, delivery, data, and reporting.

## Decision guide

| Your situation | Recommended starting point | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| You can name one broken sales, intake, or workflow process and have a defined outcome | **Foundation CRM** | A focused build can solve a contained bottleneck quickly with a practical launch and handover |
| Lead follow-up, client delivery, reporting, and handoffs are all disconnected | **Revenue & Operations System** | The business needs a connected operating system, not isolated point automations |
| You need AI to interpret documents, route leads, support customers, or use internal knowledge | **AI-Enabled Operations** | AI should be integrated with workflow, quality controls, and human escalation—not deployed as a standalone novelty |
| The problems are real, but the priorities, data flow, current tools, or expected return are unclear | **AI Business Audit** | A paid diagnostic produces an evidence-based roadmap before implementation spend |
| A system is live but needs new use cases, adoption support, monitoring, governance, or iterative improvement | **Ongoing Optimisation** | Continuous improvement protects the original investment and creates capacity for the next high-value workflow |

## Recommended commercial refinements

The company does **not** need a large number of additional packages. More packages would increase choice friction and blur the difference between core offers. Instead, retain the existing five-step ladder and clarify two combinations that improve conversion without altering the offer structure.

| Combination | When to recommend it | Commercial purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Audit → Foundation CRM** | A prospect has one suspected bottleneck but weak process clarity or unreliable data | Converts uncertainty into a low-risk paid first step, then a focused implementation |
| **Audit → Revenue & Operations System → Ongoing Optimisation** | A multi-team business has interconnected sales, delivery, reporting, or integration problems | Creates a phased transformation path with a clear post-launch relationship |

### Optional named service: Systems Health Check

If the business needs an additional entry point, offer a **Systems Health Check** as a tightly packaged version of the existing audit for businesses with a live CRM or automation estate. It should not be marketed as a separate broad service line; it is a clear audit variant.

A Systems Health Check would examine workflow reliability, data quality, unused automations, error visibility, permissions, ownership, dashboard relevance, and the next three improvement opportunities. The deliverable is a prioritised improvement plan and debrief. The price and final scope should match the commercial approach used for the existing AI Business Audit.

## Language that sells the outcome, not the tool

When describing packages, lead with the business result and then explain the tools. The following statements are specific enough for prospects and answer engines to interpret without keyword stuffing.

| Instead of leading with… | Lead with… |
| --- | --- |
| “We do monday.com and Make automations.” | “We build lead-to-delivery systems that connect CRM, follow-up, operations, and reporting.” |
| “We build AI chatbots.” | “We build AI-assisted customer, document, lead, and knowledge workflows with human safeguards.” |
| “We integrate APIs.” | “We connect the tools where your team is re-entering data, losing context, or waiting for updates.” |
| “We provide consulting.” | “We map the process, build the system, train the team, and measure whether it improves operations.” |
